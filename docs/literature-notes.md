# Working literature notes (8 November 2026)

These are scope notes, not an exhaustive novelty review. Read full methods and compare before claiming a gap.

- Ying et al., [Delegated Misalignment](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.398/) (EMNLP 2026): compares roles and delegation across six frontier models and 49 hazardous tasks; responsibility diffusion and subordinate role bias already establish that individual refusal need not compose. Our pilot does not reproduce their agent pipeline and cannot claim this phenomenon is new.
- Safin et al., [Trust propagation and structural containment](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17648) (2026 preprint): evaluates a multi-agent pipeline with signed task-bound tokens and an independent policy oracle. The paper explicitly distinguishes compromised intermediate judgment from execution-level containment. Our simple gate overlaps its structural authorization idea; any proposed protocol must be compared against a gate with equal evidence and budget.
- Liu et al., [Operational Reframing and Approval-Framed Delegation](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07097) (2026): separates framing, planner behavior, and delegation with controlled contrasts, and reports prompt sensitivity. Our present wording comparison changes both framing and resource inclusion, so cannot identify the causal factor; design a factorial control, rather than presenting prompt sensitivity itself as novel.
- Simhi et al., [ManagerBench](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/b8330f5b70b3c53172417deac6f057b1-Abstract-Conference.html) (ICLR 2026): evaluates safety versus pragmatism with human-validated scenarios and control cases. Over-refusal and safety–utility trade-offs are not new; multi-agent false-veto propagation and evidence scarcity require separate demonstration.

Potential distinction to test, not an established gap: how adversarial objections allocate a limited verification budget, and whether recovery retains provenance and action scope when evidence is incomplete or stale. This requires credible adversarial cases, explicit cost budgets, matched baselines, and a dedicated search for prior work on budgeted verification and denial of service. The current toy pilot does not establish novelty or publication readiness.
- Ge-Wang et al., [Attack Selection in Agentic AI Control Evaluations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06529) (2026 preprint): studies attackers choosing when to attack under a limited audit budget; budget-aware adversarial selection is already studied. A veto-flooding experiment would need to distinguish its threat model and measure actual queue and availability effects.
- Cuadros and Maiga, [Ambient Persuasion in a Deployed AI Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00055) (2026 preprint): reports a case including the override of an oversight agent's prior negative decision and argues for enforceable refusal persistence. Refusal persistence is therefore not an original claim here; compare against its operational case and limitations.
