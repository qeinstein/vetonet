# Experimental inventory and defensible conclusions

Reviewed 2026-11-08. This inventory separates actual model interaction from transcript replay and scripted workload. Artifacts remain in `results/`; unlike the submission paper, this file records the broader project history.

| Experiment family | What was generated by a model | Strongest supportable result | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial authorization prompts and forced-choice pilots | Independent model answers; later rules replayed those answers | Output format, prompt framing, and forced-choice scoring materially change apparent completion/refusal rates. | Interactive delegation, adaptive attacks, or a safety guarantee. Replays are not independent agent trajectories. |
| Verification-budget and queue replays | Some critic labels; much of the queue and injected objection workload is simulator-created | Under the specified synthetic world, extra scripted work can reduce completion under a fixed budget. | An attacker eliciting new work from agents; a general scheduling advantage; a naturally occurring cascade. The “same world” model comparisons are not independent replications. |
| Cascade v1/v2 | Some follow-up text, with strict-schema failures and exact-key evidence lookup | A few Qwen paths in the no-objection condition were ordinary checks of separate policy predicates. | Propagation from an unsupported objection. SmolLM2 outputs were partly malformed; each version used a fixed toy world. |
| Cascade v3.0–v3.2 diagnostics | Structured follow-up requests from model calls | Exposed queue-visibility, evidence-coverage, duplicate-check, truncation, and condition-wording confounds. | Causal inference. v3.2 used evaluator-revealing condition wording and is excluded from comparisons. |
| Cascade v3.3 smokes | All follow-up tasks were emitted by Qwen2.5-3B or SmolLM2-1.7B calls | Across two authorized paired worlds per model, the unsupported objection created no follow-up at its root. The Qwen condition had an all-decline floor; the two-world SmolLM2 completion contrast reversed direction and is uninterpretable. | The existence or absence of cascades generally. The evidence bundle was fully visible and prompts discouraged rechecking. |
| Cascade v4 final baseline arm | Every post-root task was model-emitted; evidence was split among five roles | In twenty no-objection authorized scenarios, Qwen2.5-3B generated 157 follow-ups, including 108 rechecks of already available keys. Sixteen scenarios reached depth ≥2, but monitor completion was 0/20. The fixed gate therefore prevented an uninterpretable causal contrast. | Whether a single objection causes additional obligations; behavior on unauthorized tasks; generalization beyond this model and synthetic policy environment. |

## Final empirical observation

The strongest observation from the completed sprint is narrow: **in this final no-objection protocol, the small tested checkpoint repeatedly re-requested evidence already present in its shared interaction record, and the system completed none of twenty authorized tasks.** The observation is descriptive and conditional on one checkpoint, one sampling seed, five role prompts using the same weights, four synthetic policy templates, JSON-constrained output, and a node/depth cap. It is a baseline feasibility failure, not a positive result for the Safety Cascade Paradox.

The candidate cascade hypothesis remains unresolved. The v3.3 diagnostic is too small and structurally unlike v4; v4's baseline completion gate failed, so no matched objection arm was run. We do not estimate a branching factor, safety–availability frontier, attack success rate, or intervention benefit. No NeurIPS/ICLR novelty claim is supported by these experiments.
